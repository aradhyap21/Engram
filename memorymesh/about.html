Problem Statement

The Keyword Crisis

Traditional memory systems rely on rigid keywords and hierarchical folders. They force you to categorize information before you even know how it connects. When you try to retrieve an idea, you have to remember exactly how you tagged it, not the context surrounding it.

This results in fragmented knowledge. Ideas sit in isolated silos. We lose the serendipitous connections that spark true insight. Memory becomes a static storage drive instead of a dynamic web.

Our Solution

Relational Graph Memory

Engram is built on a fundamentally different architecture. By utilizing a knowledge graph combined with advanced AI vector embeddings, Engram doesn't just store text—it stores relationships.

When you input a thought, the AI automatically analyzes the semantic meaning, extracts entities, and creates edges connecting it to your existing knowledge base. You retrieve memories not by guessing a keyword, but by exploring the neighborhood of concepts.

Architecture

Beyond Standard RAG

Traditional Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems rely almost entirely on simple vector similarity. This often retrieves fragmented, isolated chunks of data without understanding the broader context or the sequence of events.

Engram transcends this limitation. By treating information as a networked graph, our system performs multi-hop traversals. It doesn't just find text that looks similar; it finds concepts that are logically connected, providing LLMs with comprehensive context and significantly reducing hallucinations.

Scale

A Knowledge OS for Enterprise

Companies generate massive amounts of unstructured data across disparate platforms—Slack, Notion, Google Drive, and internal wikis. Engram acts as an intelligent operating system layer that sits on top of this scattered information.

It continuously ingests, structures, and links your company's data into a unified, queryable nervous system. Teams no longer search for documents; they query an active, organizational brain that surfaces insights faster than any traditional search engine.
